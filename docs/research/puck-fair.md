## The key point

There is no single verified origin story. The fair is documented back to 1613, evidence suggests an August fair existed before that, and the famous story of the goat warning the town of Cromwell's army is a legend, not established history.

## Three layers

1. **Ancient Celtic layer:** Lughnasa, the harvest, fertility and the symbolic goat.
2. **Historical fair layer:** a documented Killorglin August fair, formally chartered in 1613.
3. **King Puck legend:** the goat who supposedly warned Killorglin about Cromwell, followed by the later tradition of crowning a real goat.

The story told today isn't necessarily the origin of Puck Fair. It's more like a mythology that grew around a much older agricultural fair.

## The Cromwell legend, as told

During Oliver Cromwell's campaign in Ireland, his soldiers were moving through the countryside around the McGillycuddy's Reeks. They came upon a herd of wild goats and scattered them. One male goat, the Puck, broke away. Instead of running into the mountains with the others, he ran towards Killorglin, exhausted and frightened. When the townspeople saw the distressed goat arrive, they realised danger was approaching. They prepared themselves, protected their livestock, and escaped the worst of the attack. They never forgot what the goat had done, began honouring him every year, and eventually made him King Puck.

The problem: there is no contemporary evidence for it, and the fair is documented before the Cromwellian period, so the story can't explain the fair's existence.

## The name

- *Puck* (Irish *poc*) = the male goat.
- Puck Fair = the fair of the male goat.
- Irish name: *Aonach an Phoic*, "the fair of the he-goat" (Wikipedia).

## What happens over the three days

The organisers describe it as Ireland's oldest festival, with roots in the Gaelic calendar, and promise 12 hours of free entertainment each day. The whole town becomes the event.

**10 August, Gathering Day:** a wild mountain goat is brought into town and becomes King Puck; a local girl, Queen Puck, crowns him; the Coronation Parade runs through the town; the traditional horse fair takes place; live music and street performers; the funfair opens; pubs and streets packed. *2026 programme: parade 5:00pm, crowning 5:30pm (check each year).*

**11 August, Fair Day:** the cattle fair, plus music, street performers, children's events, food, pubs, dancing and the funfair. The cattle fair operates under the historic 1613 charter and is not organised by the modern Puck Fair committee.

**12 August, Scattering Day:** family events and street entertainment continue; King Puck is dethroned around 6pm and returned to the mountains; the festival ends with a midnight fireworks display.

**In the streets:** Irish traditional music, rock and contemporary bands, street performers, circus acts, puppets, food and market stalls, children's activities, a large travelling funfair, horse and cattle traders, packed pubs, people socialising in the streets.

**King Puck himself:** caught in the mountains by a goat handler, crowned by the Queen, and placed on the elevated Puck Stand overlooking the town for the three days, then dethroned on the third day and taken home.

## A broad sketch of how it grew

Ancient Ireland (Lughnasa, harvest, August gatherings, goat symbolism) → 1613 royal patent for an August fair → 1700s–1800s an important livestock and commercial gathering → early 1800s the goat becomes associated with the fair and takes on the role of King → 1840s onward the crowning is regularly reported → today a three-day town festival.

## Storytelling direction (owner's notes)

Puck Fair is more interesting as a story about a town than about a goat: a medieval Irish market became a harvest festival, became a livestock fair, became a three-day party, and somewhere along the way a goat became King. The strongest angle for the site is to make visitors feel what Killorglin becomes during those three days, rather than presenting it as a conventional historical festival.
