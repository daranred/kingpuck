King Puck

The character at the centre of Puck Fair: a wild goat from the mountains, crowned on Gathering Day and King of Killorglin for three days.

Not a Mascot. A Monarch.

Each year's King is a wild male goat, a poc, caught in the mountains around Killorglin by a goat handler in the weeks before the fair.

On Gathering Day he is paraded through the town, crowned by Queen Puck, and placed on the Puck Stand above the Square, where he presides over the fair until Scattering Day. On the third day he is dethroned and returned to the mountains.

The fair came first; the King came later. Regular reports of a goat being crowned only begin in the 1840s, two centuries after the fair's earliest records. How he got the crown.

The Crowning

The coronation is the centrepiece of Gathering Day. The parade leaves the bridge at five, crosses the Square and climbs Upper Bridge Street, and at half past five Queen Puck places the crown on the goat's head before the crowd at the Puck Stand. He is then raised onto his stand.

For three days, the King looks down on the fair. It is the image most people carry home from Killorglin.

Welfare, and the Way Home

It's the question every visitor asks. After the fair the King is taken down from his stand on Scattering Day, paraded back through the town, and returned to the mountains he came from.

The organisers say the welfare of the goat is of the utmost importance to everyone involved, with strict protocols in place that are overseen and checked by an independent veterinary surgeon. Documented
