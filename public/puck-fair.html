Puck Fair

Killorglin, County Kerry, 10–12 August, every year. Ireland's oldest festival, and the only one with a goat for a King. In Irish, Aonach an Phoic: the fair of the he-goat.

Three DaysCoronationProgrammeMusic & EntertainmentTraditionsGetting ThereWhere to Stay

Gathering · Fair · Scattering

For three days the whole town is the event: a horse fair and a cattle fair, music on three stages, street performers, a travelling funfair that has come for over fifty years, markets, children's events, pubs open until three in the morning, and twelve hours of free entertainment a day. In the middle of it all, a wild goat on a high stand, crowned by a local girl. Each day has its own name and its own job.

10Aug

Gathering Day

The horse fair runs from early morning at Evan's Field on the Tralee Road. In the late afternoon the Coronation Parade crosses the bridge and climbs through the Square, and Queen Puck crowns the goat King at the Puck Stand. The funfair opens, the music starts, and the town fills.

11Aug

Fair Day

The cattle fair on Langford Street, a traditional street fair still held under the 1613 charter and run separately from the festival committee. Family events in Library Place: the Bonny Baby and the Fancy Dress. Music, street shows, food, pubs and dancing all day and most of the night.

12Aug

Scattering Day

The Pet Show in the Square, more street entertainment and music. Around six o'clock the King is dethroned, paraded back down through the town and returned to the mountains. The fair ends with fireworks at midnight.

The Crowning of the King

The coronation is the heart of Gathering Day. The parade leaves the bridge at five, follows Lower Bridge Street into the Square and on up Upper Bridge Street, and at half past five Queen Puck crowns the goat before the crowd at the Puck Stand. He is raised onto the stand and reigns there until Scattering Day.

Coronation Day has its own anthem. An Poc ar Buile, the mad puck goat, sung at the crowning by Seán Ó Sé for decades, became a Puck Fair institution. Documented

The Sound of the Fair

Three stages run all day: the Puck Stand itself, Library Place, and a stage truck in the Square for the evening headliners. Young musicians open each day, walkabout acts work the streets between them, and the pubs carry the sessions through to closing time at three.

The 72-hour tradition has a history of its own. In the nineteenth century the town's few public houses could not hold the crowds, so breweries pitched tents for the fair, and the pubs put up the cattle dealers the nights before and after. The round-the-clock opening that grew from this was only trimmed to a three o'clock close in 1968. Documented

Getting There

Killorglin is on the Ring of Kerry, the N72 from Killarney and the N70 from Tralee, with Kerry Airport at Farranfore a short drive away. During the fair a shuttle bus runs from Killarney.

Follow the stewards' signs for parking. The main car parks are on the Annadale Road opposite the Bianconi Inn, on the Killarney Road near the Puck goat statue, and on the Tralee Road; expect to pay in cash. There is no camping in the town, and the nearest sites are at Inch and Glencar.

Accommodation in Killorglin books out long before August, so look early and widen the search to the mid-Kerry area and Killarney.
