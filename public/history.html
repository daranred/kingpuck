History

Four hundred years of the King

Puck Fair is often called Ireland's oldest fair. Here's what we know, what we think we know, and the legends in between.

Where it all began

Every August, the town of Killorglin on the banks of the River Laune hosts Puck Fair. At its heart is a simple, gloriously strange ritual: a wild mountain goat, a poc in Irish, is brought down from the hills, crowned King, and placed on a high stand above the town square, where he presides over three days of trading, music and celebration.

The fair's exact origins are lost to time. Many historians link it to Lughnasa, the Celtic festival marking the start of the harvest in early August, when the male goat was a symbol of fertility and plenty. The fair may simply be the latest form of a gathering that is far older than any written record.

The legend of the goat

The most famous story says that in the 1600s, Cromwellian soldiers were plundering the countryside around the Kerry mountains. A herd of goats fled before them, and one he-goat broke away and ran into Killorglin, alerting the townspeople in time to protect themselves. The town honours that goat to this day.

The fair today

Puck Fair still runs over three days. On Gathering Day the goat is paraded through the streets and crowned by the Queen of Puck, traditionally a local schoolgirl. Fair Day brings the traditional horse and cattle fair, and Scattering Day ends the reign. After the fair, the King is returned to the mountains.

Between the ceremonies there's street music, dancing, markets, fireworks and a town packed with visitors from all over the world, along with plenty of locals who wouldn't miss it.

At a glance

Puck Fair

Timeline

The King through the ages

  1. Ancient

    Lughnasa roots

    Early-August harvest gatherings in Kerry, which many believe are the fair's distant ancestor.

  2. 1613

    The charter

    A royal charter grants Killorglin the right to hold a fair. It is the oldest written record connected to the fair.

  3. 1600s

    The legend

    A he-goat runs ahead of Cromwellian soldiers and warns the town, according to the most-told origin story.

  4. 1800s

    Tolls and a loophole

    When a ban on fair tolls threatened the fair, local tradition says it was promoted as a goat fair to stay in business. Some versions of the story involve Daniel O'Connell.

  5. 1900s

    A festival for everyone

    The fair grows into one of Ireland's best-known festivals, drawing visitors, musicians and writers from home and abroad.

  6. Today

    Long live the King

    Three days of parades, music and the horse fair, with a new King crowned every August.
