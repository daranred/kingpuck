For Three Days Every August,
a Town Becomes the Fair.

Every August, Killorglin fills with horse traders, musicians, street performers, families and crowds many times the size of the town, and crowns a wild mountain goat its King. Puck Fair is one of Ireland's oldest fairs: a tradition of history, legend and community in the heart of Kerry.

A History in Motion

400+ years of Puck. 1613 is the earliest surviving written record, not necessarily the beginning.

  1. Before 1613Unknown

    An August gathering that may reach back to Lughnasa, the Celtic harvest festival.

  2. 1613Documented

    A royal patent grants Jenkyn Conway a fair on Lammas Day and the two days after.

  3. 1808Tradition

    A toll loophole, a young Daniel O'Connell, and a goat hoisted onto a platform.

  4. 1840sDocumented

    Reports of a goat actually crowned King become consistent.

  5. TodayDocumented

    A new King and Queen every 10 August, and three days the whole town turns over to the fair.

Gathering · Fair · Scattering

Gathering Day

10 August

The town fills. The goat is paraded through the streets and crowned King by the Queen of Puck.

Fair Day

11 August

The traditional horse and livestock fair, music, trade and the town at full volume.

Scattering Day

12 August

The King comes down from his stand. The fair comes to an end, but the stories live on.
