King Puck

A wild goat from the mountains, crowned for three days.

In this sectionThe Crowned GoatKings Through the YearsGalleryCoronationWhat Happens to the Goat?

Not a Mascot. A Monarch.

Each year's King is a wild male goat, a puck, taken from the mountains around Killorglin in the weeks before the fair.

On Gathering Day he is paraded through the town, crowned, and placed on a tall stand above the square, where he presides over the fair until Scattering Day.

A Royal Register

We're building a year-by-year record of every King we can document: photographs, where he came from, and who caught him.

The Register Is Being Compiled

If you have a photograph of King Puck from any year, especially before 1990, it could fill a gap in the record.

The King, Through the Lens

Gallery spaces fill as photographs are contributed and cleared for display.

The Crowning

The coronation is the centrepiece of Gathering Day. After a parade through the streets, the Queen of Puck places a crown on the goat's head before the crowd in the square. He is then raised onto his stand.

For three days, the King looks down on the fair. It is the image most people carry home from Killorglin.

Coronation at the fair

He Goes Home

It's the question every visitor asks. After the fair the King is taken down from his stand and returned to the mountains he came from.

The goat's welfare has rightly become part of the conversation around Puck. We're preparing a full account of how the King is cared for before, during and after his reign, drawn from the people responsible for him.

Long live King Puck.
