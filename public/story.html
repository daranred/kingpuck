The Story

What we know, what has been passed down, and what is legend, clearly labelled.

In this sectionWhat Is Puck Fair?HistoryWhy a Goat?LegendThe Three Days

A town, a goat and three days in August

Puck Fair takes place every year from 10 to 12 August in Killorglin, County Kerry, on the banks of the River Laune. It is one of Ireland's oldest fairs.

At its centre is a ritual found nowhere else: a wild mountain goat, a poc in Irish, is brought into town, crowned King by a young Queen, and placed on a high stand above the square for the duration of the fair.

Around the King there is a horse and livestock fair, street music, markets, late nights and a town many times its usual size.

Four centuries, labelled honestly

This archive separates documented history from tradition, theory and folklore. Every claim on the timeline carries one of four labels.

Documented

Supported by records or reliable sources.

Tradition

Passed down through generations.

Legend

Associated with Puck, not established as fact.

Unknown

The evidence doesn't allow a confident answer.

  1. Before 1613
    Unknown

    Older than the records?

    Many people connect Puck to Lughnasa, the Celtic festival marking the start of harvest in early August. It's a reasonable theory, but no record links the two directly.

  2. 1613
    Documented

    The earliest written evidence

    A charter of 1613 is the earliest surviving written record connected to the fair. It shows the fair existed by then. It does not tell us when it began.

  3. 1600s
    Legend

    The goat who warned the town

    The best-known origin story: a he-goat fled ahead of Cromwellian soldiers and raised the alarm in Killorglin. Read the legend.

  4. 1800s
    Tradition

    Tolls and a loophole

    Local tradition says that when a ban on fair tolls threatened the fair, it carried on as a goat fair. Some tellings involve Daniel O'Connell. We are looking for records.

  5. 1900s
    Documented

    A festival known far beyond Kerry

    Puck draws visitors, musicians, photographers and writers from across Ireland and abroad.

  6. Today
    Documented

    The tradition continues

    A new King and Queen every August, and the three days still run their course.

Have a source, a document or a correction? Tell us. The archive improves with every one.

The honest answer: nobody knows for sure

The goat has been a symbol of fertility and plenty in many cultures, and a male goat would make sense at a harvest-time gathering. Theory

Others point to the legend of a goat that warned the town of danger, and say the crown is an act of thanks. Legend

What is certain is that the goat is wild, from the mountains around Killorglin, and that the town has crowned one for a very long time.

The mountain, the goat, the warning Legend

The story is told like this. In the 1600s, soldiers were raiding the countryside below the mountains. The goats scattered before them. One he-goat broke from the herd and ran, without stopping, all the way into Killorglin.

His arrival warned the people that something was coming, and they had time to prepare. Ever since, the town has honoured a goat by making him King.

There is no record that this happened. It is the most-told story about Puck, and stories are part of the archive too. More legends and lore.
